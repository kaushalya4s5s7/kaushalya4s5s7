Most of Web3's hard problems aren't cryptographic — they're about deciding who's allowed to do what, and proving it after the fact. That's the thread through everything below.

**Where I found the real problem** — Building T-REX (ERC-3643) compliance infra at TheOpenAssets across four chains, I realized "compliant" isn't a checkbox, it's a system that has to hold state correctly under async execution. Most teams fake this. I got interested in not faking it.

**Where I built for real** — Founding engineer at PayZoll: payroll infra with real settlement, real money, zero tolerance for "mostly works." Rust/Soroban on Stellar, EVM on the side.

**Where I am now** — Building an agentic policy layer: permissioning, authorization, and audit trail for autonomous AI agents transacting on-chain. Also building a payment gateway from scratch. Same instinct as always — an agent (or a person) should only be able to do what it's actually allowed to do, and there should be a trail proving it did.
